## Fourteen People, Forty Projects Waiting

The client, a mid-sized rooftop EPC contractor that asked to stay unnamed, had fourteen employees and a pipeline of commercial rooftops stretching from Sector 18 to Manesar and Sohna Road. The founder was still climbing onto roofs for site surveys himself.

Two design engineers handled every drawing, and net-metering files sat unattended for weeks. The goal set at the start was ambitious: more than fifty people within nine months, spread across design, installation, DISCOM liaison, and operations and maintenance.

## Where Hiring Was Quietly Draining the Business

Job postings brought volume, not quality. Of the last five installers hired, three left within ninety days after a competitor offered a slightly better package. A so-called senior designer could not produce a basic string layout.

Interview cycles dragged on for four to five weeks, and by the time an offer went out, strong candidates had already accepted elsewhere. The costs were easy to see on the shop floor: crews waiting for supervisors, subcontractors hired at premium day rates, and rework that ate into already thin margins.

## Handing Hiring to a Specialist

The turning point came when the founder stopped treating recruitment as an admin chore and gave it to a [**Solar Recruitment Agency Gurgaon**](https://www.job24by7.com/supplychain-job-opening-register) with recruiters who had already placed engineers and technicians in energy projects across Delhi NCR. - Field and installation: supervisors, electricians and technicians certified for working at height
- Liaison and operations: net-metering executives, DISCOM coordinators and O&M engineers

Each track had its own salary benchmarks, its own assessment, and its own timeline, which stopped the founder from judging a liaison executive by the same yardstick as a roof technician.

## A Screening Process Candidates Could Actually See

Every profile moved through the same five stages: résumé review, telephonic or video screening, an assignment test, detailed candidate insights, and finally salary negotiation.

The assignment test did the heaviest lifting. Designers were handed a shadow-analysis sheet and asked to produce a 100 kWp layout, while technicians were assessed on wiring practice and fall-protection discipline.

Certificates, past employers, and references were checked before any profile reached the founder’s desk, so interviews became conversations about fit rather than fact-checking. Candidates knew the sequence in advance, which kept dropouts low, and salary expectations were settled openly before offers were rolled out, not after.

## Lessons Worth Borrowing

Hire for the roof, not the résumé. A confident interview means little until someone has drawn a layout or tightened a clamp under supervision. Give liaison roles the same respect as technical ones, because a delayed net-metering approval stalls revenue just as surely as a missing inverter.

Watch the skills arriving in 2026: hybrid solar with battery storage, drone-assisted surveys, AI-supported design tools, and performance monitoring dashboards are quickly becoming baseline expectations.

Treat retention as part of hiring too: an honest salary conversation on day one costs far less than a replacement search in month three. Finally, keep a warm bench of vetted candidates, because the next large order will not wait for a fresh round of advertising.

Design engineers with PVsyst and AutoCAD skills, plus net-metering liaison executives, are the hardest to find. Both roles need hands-on exposure that fresh graduates often lack. Installers trained for working at height are also scarce during peak commissioning months, so early planning helps.

Yes, because certificates alone do not reveal on-site habits. A short practical test on wiring, torque, and fall protection shows real competence within minutes. It also reduces early attrition, since candidates join knowing the standard the employer expects from them.
